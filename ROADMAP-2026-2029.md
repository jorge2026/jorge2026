# Professional Roadmap 2026–2029

**Target direction:** Enterprise Architect  
**Next career step:** Lead Architect responsibilities  
**Development model:** Balanced — Enterprise Architecture, Cloud, AI, FinOps, business transformation, governance, and leadership  
**Master's degree target start:** 2028  
**Target market:** International

> This roadmap is a development plan, not a claim that the listed certifications, responsibilities, or outcomes have already been achieved. Certification choices and eligibility must be checked against the issuing organizations before booking exams.

## Professional positioning

**Enterprise Architecture · Cloud · AI**

Building scalable, intelligent, and resilient enterprise ecosystems.

The goal is to connect business strategy, processes, applications, data, cloud platforms, AI, governance, and financial value.

## Roadmap

### Phase 1 — November–December 2026: Consolidate the foundation

- Strengthen AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, and OCI architecture fundamentals.
- Deepen Kubernetes, Terraform, cloud-native design, security, and reliability.
- Continue practical Generative AI and agentic architecture work.
- Establish FinOps fundamentals and cost/value measurement.
- Improve professional English through technical writing, listening, speaking, and interview practice.
- Publish architecture case studies with diagrams, assumptions, trade-offs, risks, and evidence.
- Keep LinkedIn and GitHub consistent with the Enterprise Architecture direction.

### Phase 2 — 2027: Enterprise Architecture and transformation

- Study for **TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Foundation**, then consider Practitioner after applying the method.
- Study **ArchiMate 3 Foundation** and progress to Practitioner through practical modeling.
- Apply **BPMN 2.0** to business-process modeling.
- Explore SAP LeanIX and SAP Business Transformation in the context of enterprise application landscapes.
- Build business capability maps, application portfolios, data flows, target architectures, and transformation roadmaps.
- Continue multicloud, AI architecture, and FinOps projects.
- Evaluate master's programs, curriculum, accreditation, cost, schedule, and career relevance before selecting one.

### Phase 3 — 2028: Governance, operating model, and master's degree

- Target the start of a relevant master's degree in 2028, subject to program selection, admission, and budget.
- Study COBIT and ITIL according to the governance and service-management responsibilities required by target roles.
- Consider BIAN only if financial-services architecture becomes a deliberate specialization.
- Build evidence of architecture governance, risk management, stakeholder alignment, and operating-model design.
- Evaluate PMP eligibility before planning the exam.
- Develop executive communication and architecture decision-making skills.

### Phase 4 — 2029: Consolidate Enterprise Architect capability

- Complete or make substantial progress in the master's program, depending on its duration.
- Demonstrate architecture ownership through real initiatives and documented outcomes.
- Build a portfolio of 4–6 substantial architecture case studies.
- Strengthen business cases, capability-based planning, architecture governance, and transformation roadmaps.
- Pursue international Enterprise Architect opportunities aligned with actual experience, language proficiency, and work authorization.

## Certification strategy

| Area | Credential or learning path | Planning guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise Architecture | TOGAF Enterprise Architecture Foundation | Primary 2027 target |
| Architecture modeling | ArchiMate 3 Foundation, then Practitioner | Pair with real models |
| Business process | BPMN 2.0 | Apply to a documented business process |
| FinOps | FinOps Certified Practitioner | Validate current catalog and exam details |
| SAP transformation | SAP LeanIX and SAP Business Transformation learning | Prioritize practical tool and portfolio work |
| Governance | COBIT Foundation | Select the current official version when scheduling |
| IT service management | ITIL Foundation | Align with operating-model responsibilities |
| Banking architecture | BIAN Foundation / Practitioner | Optional; only if banking is a target vertical |
| Project leadership | PMP | Verify PMI education and project-leadership eligibility first |
| Business analysis | CBAP | Only after meeting IIBA work-history and professional-development requirements |

Do not pursue every credential at once. Select certifications that reinforce current responsibilities and the next target role.

## Personal brand and international career

- Position the LinkedIn headline and About around Enterprise Architecture, Cloud, AI, and business value.
- Keep the profile evidence-based: use current job titles and do not imply an Enterprise Architect title before holding it.
- Publish two or three useful technical posts per month in English, starting with topics that can be explained confidently.
- Publish one deeper architecture case study or technical article per quarter.
- Build relationships with Enterprise Architects, Principal Architects, CTOs, architecture recruiters, and cloud/AI leaders.
- Target Lead Cloud Architect, Lead Solutions Architect, Cloud/AI Architect, and progressively Enterprise Architect roles.
- Verify remote-work eligibility, country restrictions, time zones, and work authorization for every international vacancy.

## English development

**Target:** progress from the user's stated basic level toward B2 by the end of 2029, with milestones adjusted after an initial level assessment.

- Daily practice in listening, speaking, vocabulary, grammar, and writing.
- Explain one architecture concept aloud each week.
- Practice technical and behavioral interviews.
- Write LinkedIn posts and repository documentation in clear, accessible English.
- Deliver quarterly architecture presentations in English.

## Quarterly scorecard

Review every quarter:

- Practical architecture artifacts completed.
- Repositories with reproducible validation.
- Certifications completed or actively prepared.
- Business outcomes and leadership responsibilities demonstrated at work.
- English speaking/listening progress.
- Relevant international professional conversations and interviews.
- Progress toward master's admission and funding.

## Guiding principle

Build evidence before claiming expertise. The strongest Enterprise Architect profile combines business understanding, architecture methods, technology depth, governance, financial judgment, communication, and demonstrated outcomes.
